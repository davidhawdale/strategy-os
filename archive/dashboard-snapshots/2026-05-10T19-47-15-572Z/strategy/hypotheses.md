# Hypothesis Register — Scottish Borders Daily Local News

**Seed source:** `strategy/problem.md` (2026-05-09, EXTENSION)
**Analogue model:** DC Thomson's *Press and Journal* — a daily regional newspaper plus app published in Aberdeen, with localised editions covering the north and north-east of Scotland. Latest certified print circulation H2 2025: **18,382 avg per issue** ([ABC](https://www.abc.org.uk/product/9327)). DC Thomson disclosed ~25-28K digital subscribers across P&J + The Courier combined ([Press Gazette, 2024](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/dc-thomson-digital-subscribers/)) — significantly behind its public 75K-by-2025 target.
**Question being tested:** Would a daily-cadence local news service generate equivalent or better financial outcomes in the Scottish Borders (~115,000 population) as the *Press and Journal* does in its catchment (Aberdeen + north-east Scotland, ~500,000+ population)?
**Pro-rata implication:** P&J print penetration ~3.5% of catchment population. Applied to Borders, this implies a print-equivalent ceiling of ~3,500-5,000 daily readers; digital adds to that base. Material recalibration of base-case unit economics.

## 1. Problem

**Confidence State:** RESEARCHED

### Claim

Local news consumers in the Scottish Borders are served by weekly print titles only, leaving a structural cadence gap between when local events occur and when residents can read about them. Existing alternatives — weekly print, BBC regional digital, and social media community groups — each fail on different dimensions (timeliness, depth, local specificity, signal-to-noise). The gap is the absence of a credible, professionally-edited daily local news product across the Borders towns axis (Galashiels, Hawick, Jedburgh, Kelso, Peebles, Selkirk, Melrose, Eyemouth, Duns).

### Desired State

Evidence that a meaningful share of Borders adults (a) experience the cadence/depth gap as a real problem, (b) currently substitute with workarounds they find unsatisfactory, and (c) would change behaviour for a daily local product.

### Current State

Structural reasoning that the cadence gap exists is strong (T1: weekly publication confirmed). Evidence that the gap is *experienced as a problem worth paying or attending to* is T2/T3 — inferred from analogue (Press and Journal viability in Aberdeen) and general UK local-news consumption data, not from Borders residents directly.

### Possibility Space

**Considered:**

1. **Cadence gap (PRIMARY)** — Borders residents have only weekly local news; daily events go unreported until 5–7 days later.
2. **Depth/investigative gap** — weeklies cover events but not investigations, council scrutiny, court reporting at depth.
3. **Discovery/aggregation gap** — local news exists across many sources (council minutes, community Facebook groups, weeklies, BBC) but is not aggregated.
4. **Civic engagement gap** — residents disengaged from local democracy because information arrives too late to act on.
5. **Trust/misinformation gap** — community Facebook groups contain unverified rumour; no professional moderation.
6. **Hyper-local commerce gap** — local businesses have no effective advertising vehicle between weekly print runs.

**Alternatives carried:**

- *Depth/investigative gap (#2)* — plausible co-problem; carry as secondary because daily product can resolve both cadence and depth simultaneously.
- *Hyper-local commerce gap (#6)* — relevant to the revenue side (advertiser problem). Carry into Unit Economics.

**Eliminated:**

- *Civic engagement gap (#4)* — eliminated as primary: hard to measure, no public signal that Borders residents articulate "I am disengaged because news is late." Reframe as downstream effect of #1.
- *Trust/misinformation gap (#5)* — eliminated as primary: real but national/universal, not Borders-specific. Would not differentially favour a daily Borders product over national fact-checked outlets.
- *Discovery/aggregation gap (#3)* — eliminated as primary: aggregation alone (no original reporting) has weak monetisation track record in UK regional digital.

## 2. Segment

**Confidence State:** RESEARCHED

### Claim

The primary segment is Scottish Borders adults aged 35+ with established local roots (residence >5 years), who already consume *some* form of local news weekly and are digitally fluent enough to use a smartphone news app. They are identifiable by postcode (TD and EH-Borders prefixes), confirmable by readership of existing weeklies and engagement with town-focused Facebook groups, and primary because they combine the highest probable willingness-to-pay with the strongest behavioural signal of existing local-news engagement.

### Desired State

Validated segment definition with observable filters, addressable size of 30,000-50,000 adults, and a clear access path (postcode targeting, existing weekly subscriber base purchase, Facebook group seeding).

### Current State

Segment is plausibly defined from public demographic data and analogue reasoning. Pain intensity within segment is T2/T3 — inferred, not measured.

### Possibility Space

**Considered:**

1. **Established Borders adults 35+ (PRIMARY)** — long-term residents, weekly readers, digitally fluent.
2. **Borders commuters** — adults working in Edinburgh but living in Borders; strong digital, possibly weak local affiliation.
3. **Borders retirees 65+** — high local affiliation, high time, weakest digital fluency.
4. **Borders incomers / lifestyle migrants** — recent movers, often from cities; high digital, variable local roots.
5. **Borders young adults 18-34** — lowest local-news engagement nationally per Ofcom; likely weak segment.
6. **Borders SMEs / advertisers** — secondary segment for the revenue side; not the consumer reader.

**Alternatives carried:**

- *Borders commuters (#2)* — distinct access path (Edinburgh transport, podcast-friendly), could justify a separate distribution motion. Carry as secondary.
- *Borders retirees (#3)* — print-leaning, lower digital — strong fit for a print component but weak for app. Carry as a constraint on product format (cannot be app-only).
- *Borders incomers (#4)* — high willingness-to-pay for connection, but smaller in size. Carry as a tertiary opportunity, not primary.

**Eliminated:**

- *Borders young adults 18-34 (#5)* — eliminated: Ofcom data shows under-35s have lowest local-news engagement nationally; pain score 1-2; not enough public signal of demand. Reactivate only if evidence emerges.
- *SMEs / advertisers (#6)* — eliminated as *consumer* segment (they are part of the revenue model, not the reader segment); recorded in Unit Economics.

## 3. Unit Economics

**Confidence State:** RESEARCHED

### Claim

At a target ARPU of £40-£75/year (consumer subscription) blended with advertising revenue of £8-£25 per reader/year, a Borders daily product with **4,000-8,000** paying readers (recalibrated from the Press & Journal pro-rata: P&J achieves ~18,382 print + ~25K digital across a catchment ~4-5× the size of the Borders) can plausibly deliver gross margins of 30-50% and a payback within 12-24 months — but only if total cost of journalism is held under £300K/year by leveraging AI-assisted production and a small (2-4 FTE) editorial team. The model fails immediately if subscription conversion of addressable audience falls below ~3%, or if AI-assisted journalism cannot meaningfully reduce per-story production cost. Bootstrap viability requires positive contribution margin within 12-15 months; venture-grade returns are unlikely at this market size.

### Desired State

Validated unit economics showing LTV:CAC > 3:1 (sustainable publishing business) and payback < 18 months with realistic assumptions on conversion, churn, and ad fill.

### Current State

All inputs T2 or T3. No customer evidence, no advertising sell-through evidence. Ranges presented; ground truth required before any input can be promoted.

### Possibility Space — Revenue Models

**Considered:**

1. **Hybrid sub + ads (PRIMARY)** — print/digital subscription as anchor revenue, advertising as secondary. Mirrors Press and Journal model.
2. **Sub-only (digital pay)** — Times-style hard paywall, no ads.
3. **Ad-only (free)** — fully free, all revenue from ads (mirrors Metro / community newspapers).
4. **Membership / patronage** — supporter-tier model, lower volume higher ARPU.
5. **B2B/civic** — sell aggregated local intelligence to councils, businesses, regional bodies.

**Alternatives carried:**

- *Membership model (#4)* — carry as Phase 2 upsell layer (founding members, supporter tier at £100-£150/yr).
- *B2B/civic (#5)* — carry as a long-term diversification; low confidence in addressable size.

**Eliminated:**

- *Sub-only (#2)* — eliminated for Borders scale: the audience is too small to support a Times-style paywall; advertising must carry meaningful share.
- *Ad-only (#3)* — eliminated: UK regional digital ad RPMs are too low to fund original journalism at the required cost base.

## 4. Value Proposition

**Confidence State:** RESEARCHED

### Claim

For long-resident Scottish Borders adults aged 35+ who currently piece together local news from weekly print, BBC regional, and noisy Facebook groups, a daily AI-assisted local news product (digital app + lightweight weekly print companion) delivers professional, town-level reporting on a daily cadence — unlike The Southern Reporter or Border Telegraph (weekly only and increasingly thin) and unlike Facebook community groups (no editorial standard, low signal). Its unique capability is daily depth at weekly cost, made viable by AI-assisted production that legacy regionals cannot match.

### Desired State

VP claim validated by: (a) >40% of segment surveyed find the claim resonant; (b) >15% express purchase intent at proposed price; (c) head-to-head messaging tests beat status-quo alternatives.

### Current State

VP is composed from research and structural reasoning. No segment validation. Each clause is traceable to an upstream hypothesis or competitive analysis but is not yet ground-tested.

## 6. Solution Design (Proposed)

**Support State:** PROPOSED
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-09

### Positioning Statement

For long-resident Scottish Borders adults 35+ who currently rely on weekly print and noisy social-media groups for local news, **The Borders Daily** (working name) is a daily AI-assisted regional news service — digital + app, with optional print companion — that delivers professional, town-level reporting at the cadence of modern news consumption. Unlike The Southern Reporter, Border Telegraph, and Hawick News (weekly only, town-fragmented, declining), and unlike Facebook community groups (unedited, low signal), The Borders Daily covers all the Borders towns daily, with editorial standards, in one place. Its unique capability is daily depth at weekly cost, made viable by AI-assisted production.

### Feature Map

| Feature                                                        | Solves Problem                   | Job Dimension                                | Priority | Tier |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---- |
| Daily news app (iOS+Android, PWA)                              | Cadence gap (#1)                 | Functional + emotional (habit, completeness) | MVP      | T2   |
| Daily email digest                                             | Cadence + low-friction discovery | Functional                                   | MVP      | T2   |
| Town-tagged feeds (Hawick / Gala / Peebles / Kelso / Jedburgh) | Town-loyalty fragmentation       | Functional + social (town pride)             | MVP      | T2   |
| Court & council reporting                                      | Depth gap (#2)                   | Functional + civic-emotional                 | MVP      | T2   |
| Community events & births/deaths                               | Hyper-local lifecycle            | Social + emotional                           | MVP      | T2   |
| Weekly print companion (digest)                                | Print-loyal segment retention    | Emotional (ritual, gift, archive)            | POST_MVP | T2   |
| Local advertiser self-serve portal                             | Advertiser revenue               | Functional (advertiser side)                 | POST_MVP | T2   |
| Reader contributions / tip line                                | Sourcing + community             | Social                                       | POST_MVP | T2   |
| AI summary / digest features                                   | Time-poor reader retention       | Functional                                   | POST_MVP | T2   |
| Audio briefings / podcast                                      | Commuter segment                 | Functional                                   | FUTURE   | T3   |
| Borders archive search                                         | Historical/deep                  | Emotional                                    | FUTURE   | T3   |

### MVP Scope (Aha Moment)

**Aha moment:** "I opened the app this morning and saw three things that happened in my town yesterday — that I would not have read about for another five days."

**Time-to-value target:** under 5 minutes from first install to first article read with town-relevance.

**MVP includes:** Daily app + daily email digest + town-tagged feeds + court/council reporting + community events. ~5 features.

**Excluded from MVP (with rationale and re-add trigger):**

- Print companion: defer — fixed cost adds risk; re-add when paid digital >5,000 confirms reader appetite for print upgrade.
- Advertiser self-serve portal: defer — manual ad sales for first 12 months; build self-serve when advertiser count >50.
- Audio: defer — production cost premium without proven commuter segment validation.

### Build vs Buy Decisions

| Component            | Build / Buy                                    | Rationale                                          |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| CMS / publishing     | Buy (Ghost, Substack-pro, or similar)          | Standard problem; do not differentiate here        |
| App                  | Buy / wrap (PWA + native wrapper)              | Cost discipline; switch to native if scale demands |
| Email                | Buy (existing ESP)                             | Commodity                                          |
| AI production stack  | Build/integrate (LLM API + editorial workflow) | This is the unique capability; must control        |
| Ad serving           | Buy (GAM or equiv.)                            | Commodity                                          |
| Subscription/paywall | Buy (Pico, Memberful, Stripe)                  | Commodity                                          |

### AI Leverage Points (Critical Risk Area)

| Point                                                                              | Use                                                     | Risk                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Story drafting from primary sources (council minutes, court lists, press releases) | Reduces journalist time/story                           | Quality, accuracy, hallucination |
| Story expansion / localisation                                                     | Same story re-angled per town                           | Repetitiveness, thin content     |
| Editorial quality checks                                                           | Spot fact errors, grammar                               | False positives -> editor cost   |
| Personalisation (town feed)                                                        | User-level content prioritisation                       | Privacy, filter bubble           |
| Automated event scraping                                                           | Local council, court, sport listings -> structured data | Source coverage gaps             |

**Critical condition:** AI must augment, never replace, editorial judgment for the trust hypothesis (V2) to hold.

### Constraints from Hypotheses

| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |

- Each town has at least one original-reporting story per day.

## 7. GTM Plan (Proposed)

**Support State:** PROPOSED
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-09

### GTM Architecture

**Pattern:** Content + Community-led with subscription conversion (Hybrid).
**Rationale:** ACV £40-£75 rules out sales-led; weak inherent virality rules out PLG-pure. The product *is* daily content; content + local community seeding is the only viable foundation.

### Channel Sequence

**Phase 1: Validation (months 0-6)**

- Channels: Founder-led community + FB community-group seeding (50%) + Existing weekly partnership / list overlap test (30%) + Direct mail to TD postcodes test (20%).
- Total budget: £25K – £60K (bootstrap-viable).
- Target KPIs:
  - Email/free signups: 2,000-4,000 by month 6.
  - Email open rate: >35% (regional news benchmark).
  - Free-to-paid conversion: 3-7%.
  - Town distribution of signups: no single town >50% of total.
- Exit gate: 2,500+ free signups, paid CR >3%, signup CAC <£15, at least one town with >500 engaged readers.
- Kill criteria: <1,000 free signups by month 6 with adequate effort -> problem or VP hypothesis is wrong; reassess. Paid CR <1% by month 6 -> price/value mismatch. CAC >£40 by month 6 -> channel-economics nonviable.

**Phase 2: Traction (months 6-18)**

- Channels: Facebook/Meta paid (40%) + Content/SEO (25%) + Community/founder (15%) + Existing partnership (10%) + Direct mail (10%).
- Target KPIs:
  - Paid subs: 2,500-5,000 by month 18.
  - Blended CAC: <£15.
  - Annual churn: <40%.
  - Ad revenue: £8+ per reader/year unlocked from month 12.
  - No single channel >60% of acquisition.
- Exit gate: 3,000+ paid subs, blended CAC <£15, churn trending toward <35%, at least 3 channels producing >500 subs each.
- Kill criteria: blended CAC rising >25% QoQ for 2 consecutive quarters; sub count flat <2,000 for 6 months; contribution margin negative at month 18.

**Phase 3: Scale (months 18-36)**

- Channels: Paid Meta (35%) + SEO/content (30%) + Paid search (15%) + Community/partnership (10%) + Print companion launch (10%).
- Target KPIs:
  - Paid subs: 6,000-9,000 by month 36.
  - LTV:CAC: >3:1 (ideally >5:1 for bootstrap).
  - Net revenue retention: >100% (upgrades + ad expansion offset churn).
  - Ad revenue per reader: £15+/yr.
- Kill criteria: NRR <100% past month 24; contribution margin <15% at month 30.

### Messaging Framework

- **Headline:** "The Borders, every day."
- **Subhead:** "Your towns, professionally reported, every morning — not next week."
- **Proof points (3 max):**
  1. Original reporting from Hawick, Galashiels, Peebles, Kelso, and Jedburgh — every day.
  2. Court, council, and community in one place.
  3. From £4/month — less than your weekly paper, every day.
- **Awareness-level CTAs:**

| Awareness                           | Lead                                        | CTA                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Unaware                             | "What's happening in your town today?"      | "See today's stories free" |
| Problem-aware (cadence frustration) | "Tired of waiting a week to read the news?" | "Try the daily"            |
| Solution-aware (comparing)          | "Daily where the others are weekly."        | "Start your free month"    |
| Most aware                          | "From £4/month. Cancel anytime."           | "Subscribe"                |

- **Channel adaptations:**
  - FB community groups: zero-marketing posts for 4-8 weeks (free articles shared as contribution); product mention only after trust earned.
  - Paid Meta: town-targeted creatives ("News from Hawick today" / "News from Peebles today") with town landmark imagery.
  - Direct mail: letterbox piece with QR -> free month, postcoded by town.
  - Existing-weekly partnership: offer to co-publish or list-swap with weekly subscribers as launch base.

### Operational Constraints

| Constraint                                                                                 | Source              | Blast  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ------ |
| Founder time required for Phase 1 community presence                                       | Growth Architecture | High   |
| £4/mo lower bound implies digital-only at this price; print is upsell tier                | Pricing             | High   |
| AI-assisted production must be reliable from day 1                                         | Solution Design     | High   |
| Borders towns need town-tagged content — single regional feed will fail town-loyalty test | Segment             | High   |

| Weekly competitors may price-cut defensively                                               | Competitive         | Low    |

### GTM Risks (Diagnostic Findings)

1. **Demand existence is T3.** Borders residents may not articulate cadence as a problem — entire plan rests on this.
2. **Channel concentration in Phase 1.** Founder-led + FB community = 65%; if either fails, Phase 1 fails.
3. **AI quality risk.** Trust loss from any high-profile AI-generated error in Phase 1 could end the project.
4. **Town fragmentation risk.** If Hawick readers reject Galashiels content (or vice versa), product structure is wrong.
5. **Advertiser CAC not stress-tested.** £8-£25/reader/yr ad revenue assumes a sales motion not yet designed.

### Constraints from Hypotheses

- From Problem: cadence-and-depth gap must be real, not aspirational.
- From Segment: 30-50K addressable; cross-Borders product viable.
- From Unit Economics: blended CAC <£15, churn <35%, 8-15K paid subs by month 24.
- From Growth Architecture: free-to-paid conversion path required; no pure paywall in Phase 1.
