## 1. Problem

**Claim:** Scottish Borders residents lack a single trusted, daily, professionally-edited local news product covering the whole region; existing provision is a small number of weekly papers and Facebook pages that publish on a slower cadence than civic life moves at. Working-age and engaged readers turn to social media for daily updates and accept the loss of editorial standards, while older readers buy the weekly but report a thinning of substantive local coverage. The structural gap is daily editorial cadence with regional breadth and town-level specificity.

**Confidence:** researched

**Possibility Space:**
- Considered:
  - P1: No daily regional news product exists for the Borders (cadence/scope gap)
  - P2: Weekly papers exist but produce thin, low-investigation content (depth/quality gap)
  - P3: News is fragmented across town-specific Facebook pages with no editorial standard (trust/aggregation gap)
  - P4: Civic accountability journalism (councils, courts, planning) is under-covered (democracy gap)
  - P5: Residents lack a sense of regional identity and want town-only news (identity gap — alternative framing)
- Alternatives carried:
  - P2 (depth/quality) — partially overlaps with P1 but is a distinct frame: the issue may be editorial investment, not cadence. Carry as alternative — the validation question of "do residents want daily, or do they want better?" is currently unresolved.
  - P4 (civic accountability) — narrower frame, structurally connected to P1 and P2. Carry; may be the most defensible "why now" angle.
- Eliminated:
  - P3 (Facebook fragmentation) — eliminated as primary frame: this is a symptom of P1/P2, not an independent problem. Residents using Facebook do so because no better daily product exists. [T2]
  - P5 (no regional identity) — eliminated: contradicted by the existing weekly Border Telegraph covering multiple towns from a regional masthead since 1896, and by the Scottish Borders Council operating as a unitary authority since 1996. Town identity is real but is not exclusive of regional identity. [T2]

**Evidence:**

## 2. Segment

**Claim:** The primary segment is *engaged Borders residents aged 40+ with a working-age stake in the region* (employed, business-owning, council-engaged, or property-owning) — people whose decisions are shaped by knowing what is happening in council, planning, business, schools, and rural affairs across the Borders. They are identifiable by current consumption of the Border Telegraph or Southern Reporter, membership of Borders-focused Facebook groups, or postcodes in TD1–TD15 and EH44–EH46. They are primary because the Borders population skews older than the Scottish average and digital adoption is sufficient (~80% superfast broadband coverage) for a hybrid print+digital product to reach them.

**Confidence:** researched

**Possibility Space:**
- Considered:
  - S1: Engaged Borders residents aged 40+ (primary)
  - S2: Younger Borders residents aged 18–39 (digital-native, mobile-first)
  - S3: Borders SME owners and managers (business-focused subscribers)
  - S4: Borders diaspora — former residents living elsewhere who want home news
  - S5: Tourists and second-home owners (transient demand)
- Alternatives carried:

  - S4 (diaspora) — small but high-margin; the Press and Journal has a measurable diaspora following via digital. Carry as expansion segment, not primary.
- Eliminated:
  - S2 (under-40s) — eliminated as primary: national base rates show news avoidance highest in 18–34 cohort; Mill Media's success notwithstanding, their subscribers skew older. Borders demographic is also disproportionately 50+. [T2]
  - S5 (tourists/second-home) — eliminated as primary: transient relationship, unlikely to pay for ongoing subscription. May read free content. [T2]

## 4. Unit Economics

**Claim:** The verdict at this stage is *contingent viability*. A digital-led hybrid model with print component could plausibly achieve LTV:CAC of ~3–6x with payback of 9–18 months if it reaches 4,000–8,000 paying subscribers at £8–12/month, plus £150K–£400K annual advertising revenue from Borders SMEs; this requires unit costs in line with a small newsroom of 4–7 journalists supported by shared DC Thomson back-office. The single most important condition is whether the Borders' segment of ~30,000–45,000 addressable households yields a 10–20% conversion at a price point similar to the Press and Journal — a T3 ground-truth question.

**Confidence:** researched

**Possibility Space:**
- Considered:
  - UE1: Subscription-led hybrid (digital primary, print Saturday/weekend) — primary
  - UE2: Subscription-only digital (newsletter/website, no print)
  - UE3: Advertising-led free product (no subscription, ad-supported only)
  - UE4: Membership model (Bristol Cable-style)
- Alternatives carried:
  - UE2 — lower fixed cost, viable if print proves uneconomic at Borders scale. Carry.
  - UE4 — possible commercial-model overlay; carry as fallback if subscription conversion is too low.
- Eliminated:
  - UE3 (ad-only free) — eliminated: structural decline of UK regional ad revenue (industry CAGR -6.1% over 5 years), and ad-only models have failed across the UK regional press. [Press Gazette — Colossal decline of UK regional media](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/regional-newspapers/colossal-decline-of-uk-regional-media-since-2007-revealed/) [T1]

**Scenario Analysis:**

*Optimistic:* 8,000 paying subs at £10/month (£960K/year) + £400K advertising = £1.36M revenue. Newsroom cost (5 journalists at avg £45K loaded = £225K, editor £65K, plus £150K production/distribution/tech with DCT shared services) ≈ £440K, leaving ~£900K contribution before central allocation. LTV:CAC ≈ 6x, payback ~9 months.

*Base:* 5,000 paying subs at £9/month (£540K/year) + £250K advertising = £790K revenue. Costs ≈ £500K (with modest print Saturday edition). Contribution ~£290K. LTV:CAC ≈ 3.5–4x, payback ~14 months.

*Pessimistic:* 2,500 paying subs at £8/month (£240K/year) + £120K advertising = £360K revenue. Costs ≈ £450K. Loss-making ~£90K/year. LTV:CAC ≈ 1.5–2x, payback >24 months. Not viable as standalone.

*Kill:* Below 2,500 paying subs after 18 months of operation, OR ARPU below £6/month sustained, OR advertising below £100K/year — economics cannot support a 4+ journalist newsroom even with DCT shared back-office.

## 5. Solution Design (Proposed)

**Support State:** PROPOSED

**Growth Architecture:** Hybrid (Traditional regional publisher + light PLG via free-to-paid digital funnel)

**Architecture Rationale:**
- ACV: £96–£144/year per subscriber → light-touch/inbound viable; sales-assisted not viable per-subscription but is viable for advertising-sales side.
- Buyer type: user = buyer for subscriptions (individual residents); committee-style for SME advertising (owner + manager).
- Time-to-value: a single day's edition with content the reader cares about — must demonstrate value within first 24–72 hours of free trial.
- Collaboration: not inherent (news consumption is solo) — limits viral growth loops; growth must be paid + earned media + cross-promotion.
- Selection: Hybrid is correct because the subscription product is light-touch digital but the advertising commercial motion is sales-assisted and the brand-trust play is media/PR-driven.

**Positioning:** *For engaged Borders residents who currently piece their daily news together from weekly papers, town Facebook groups and word of mouth, [Product] is a daily local news service for the Scottish Borders that delivers the whole region — every town, every day — with the editorial standard of the Press and Journal. Unlike the weekly Border Telegraph and Southern Reporter, it moves at the pace of civic life; unlike Facebook, it is edited and accountable. Its unique capability is bringing the DC Thomson community-editions model — proven daily across the north of Scotland — to a region currently served only by weekly cadence.*

Category framing: "Daily local news service for the Scottish Borders" — note: "newspaper" framing is rejected because it constrains the product to print; "platform" framing is rejected because it overpromises tech sophistication. [GOVERNOR_DECISION: brand/product name — out of scope of this skill]

## 7. GTM Plan (Proposed)

**Support State:** PROPOSED
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-10

### GTM Architecture
- Pattern: Hybrid (digital-led subscriber acquisition + sales-led advertiser acquisition + earned media for trust)
- Rationale: ACV per subscriber is light-touch but the parallel advertising motion is sales-assisted; both motions run in parallel from Day 1 because the Unit Economics base case depends on both subscription and advertising revenue.

### Channel Sequence

**Phase 1: Validation (months 0–9, soft launch in 1–2 towns)**
- Channels: DCT cross-promotion (30%), Borders Facebook/Meta paid (35%), direct mail in TD postcodes (20%), local PR + community events (10%), partnerships (5%).
- Total budget: estimated £100K–£200K (T3) marketing in Phase 1; assumes leverage of DCT existing channels.
- Target KPIs (Phase 1):
  - Free-tier signup rate from cross-promotion: 2–5% of impressions [T3 — benchmark from DCT cross-promo data, governor to confirm].
  - Free-to-paid conversion: 3–6% (Mill Media / P&J range) [T2].
  - Cost-per-paying-subscriber: <£40 [T2].
  - 500 paying subscribers by month 6, 1,500 by month 12.
- Exit gate (to Phase 2): paying subs ≥ 1,500 AND CAC ≤ £40 AND blended retention ≥ 85% month-3.
- Kill criteria:
  - Paying subs <500 at month 9 with >£60K spent: reassess segment or product framing.
  - CAC >£80 at month 9 across all channels: economics infeasible at projected ARPU.

**Phase 2: Traction (months 9–24, region-wide rollout)**
- Channels: DCT cross-promotion (20%), Borders Facebook/Meta paid (30%), direct mail (20%), community/PR (10%), partnerships (10%), paid search (10%).
- Target KPIs:
  - Paying subs growth: 4,000 by month 24.
  - Net revenue retention: >100%.
  - Advertising revenue: >£200K run-rate by month 24.
  - Blended CAC: stable or declining QoQ.
- Exit gate (to Phase 3): paying subs ≥ 4,000, advertising ≥ £200K, no single channel >70% of acquisition.
- Kill criteria:
  - Blended CAC rising >25% QoQ over 2 quarters with no offsetting LTV improvement.
  - Advertising stuck below £100K at month 24.

**Phase 3: Scale (months 24–48)**
- Channels: full mix plus compounding SEO (40%), Meta + paid search (30%), DCT cross-promotion (10%), partnerships/PR (10%), experimental/audio (10%).
- Target KPIs:
  - 6,000–8,000 paying subs, £300K+ advertising, contribution-positive.
- Kill criteria:
  - Contribution remains negative after 36 months from launch with no clear path under management's view of base-case assumptions.

### Messaging Framework
- Headline: *The whole Borders, every day.*
- Subhead: *Daily local news for Hawick, Galashiels, Peebles, Kelso, Jedburgh and the towns and villages between. Edited. Accountable. Trusted.*
- Proof points (3):
  - Daily reporting on Scottish Borders Council, courts, and planning (no other Borders source does this daily).
  - Town-level tagging — your town, every day, on every device.
  - Brought to you by the team behind the Press and Journal — the UK's best-selling regional daily.
- Awareness-level CTAs:
  - Unaware: "What's happening in your town this week? Find out free for 30 days."
  - Problem-aware: "Stop scrolling Facebook for Borders news. Get the edited daily."
  - Solution-aware: "Try the Borders Daily free for 30 days — cancel any time."
  - Product-aware: "Subscribe £9/month — first month free."
  - Most aware: "Subscribe now."
- Channel adaptations:
  - DCT cross-promo: lean on the P&J / Courier credibility transfer.
  - Borders Facebook / Meta: lead with specific reporting — a single planning story, a specific council vote — not generic "local news" language.

  - Community PR: editor presence at Common Ridings, agricultural shows, council meetings — visible trust-building.
